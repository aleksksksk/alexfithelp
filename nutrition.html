MASS GAIN

Bulking (Muscle Gain)

To build muscle mass, you need a consistent calorie surplus and proper macronutrient distribution: protein, fats, and carbohydrates.

The basic principle is simple — you eat more than you burn, but in a structured way, not randomly. Calories provide energy and mass, protein builds muscle, fats support hormones, and carbohydrates fuel strength and performance.

In simple terms, bulking is a controlled “slight surplus” every day — not overeating.

Macronutrient Breakdown

  • Protein: ~2g per kg of body weight (muscle building material)
  • Fats: ~0.9g per kg (hormonal support, testosterone balance)
  • Carbohydrates: remaining calories (energy & training performance)

Muscle growth is not about perfect food — it is about consistency. Stable calories, stable protein intake, and no long-term deficits.

FAT LOSS

Cutting (Body Fat Reduction)

Cutting is the process of reducing body fat percentage while preserving as much muscle mass as possible. The foundation of this strategy is a negative energy balance, where energy expenditure exceeds calorie intake.

Calorie balance is the key factor that determines the speed and quality of fat loss.

Nutrition Principles

  • Protein: muscle preservation and anti-catabolic protection
  • Fats: hormonal function and overall health
  • Carbohydrates: adjustable energy source reduced over time

From a nutrition science perspective, optimal macronutrient ranges during cutting are:

  • Protein: 2.0–2.5 g per kg body weight
  • Fats: 0.7–0.9 g per kg body weight
  • Carbohydrates: remaining calories after protein and fats

A calorie deficit of 15–25% from maintenance is generally optimal. More aggressive deficits increase the risk of muscle loss and strength decline.

WEIGHT LOSS

Weight Loss (Fat Reduction)

Weight loss is the process of reducing total body weight through a calorie deficit, where the body uses fat stores as its primary energy source.

Unlike competitive “cutting,” this approach focuses not only on preserving muscle mass but also on sustainable and comfortable long-term weight reduction without extreme restrictions.

Calorie balance is the main regulator of body weight — if you consume fewer calories than you burn, weight decreases.

Nutrition Principles

  • Moderate calorie deficit: −10% to −20% of maintenance
  • Focus on sustainability and long-term adherence
  • High satiety foods to reduce hunger

From a nutrition science perspective, macronutrients are distributed as follows:

  • Protein: slightly elevated for muscle preservation and satiety
  • Fats: not overly restricted to maintain hormonal health
  • Carbohydrates: adjusted based on activity level and deficit size

Macro Guidelines

  • Protein: 1.6–2.2 g per kg body weight
  • Fats: 0.8–1.0 g per kg body weight
  • Carbohydrates: remaining calories
