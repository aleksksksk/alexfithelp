Mass gain

Bulking (Muscle Gain)

To build muscle mass, you need a consistent calorie surplus and proper macronutrient distribution: protein, fats, and carbohydrates.

The basic principle is simple: eat more than you burn, but in a structured way, not randomly. Calories provide energy and mass, protein builds muscle, fats support hormones, and carbohydrates fuel performance.

In simple terms, bulking is a controlled slight surplus every day, not overeating.

Protein Around 2 g per kg of body weight for muscle building material.
Fats Around 0.9 g per kg for hormonal support and balance.
Carbohydrates The remaining calories for energy and training output.

Muscle growth is not about perfect food. It is about consistency, stable calories, and stable protein intake.

Fat loss

Cutting (Body Fat Reduction)

Cutting is the process of reducing body fat percentage while preserving as much muscle mass as possible. The foundation is a negative energy balance.

Calorie balance is the main factor that determines the speed and quality of fat loss.

Protein Supports muscle retention and anti-catabolic protection.
Fats Support hormonal function and overall health.
Carbohydrates Act as the adjustable energy source during the deficit.
A calorie deficit of 15 to 25 percent from maintenance is usually the best middle ground. Larger deficits increase the risk of muscle loss and strength decline.

Weight loss

Weight Loss (Fat Reduction)

Weight loss is the process of reducing total body weight through a calorie deficit, where the body uses stored energy to cover the gap.

Unlike aggressive cutting, this approach focuses on sustainability and comfortable long-term progress.

If you consistently consume fewer calories than you burn, body weight trends downward.

Moderate deficit Usually 10 to 20 percent below maintenance.
Adherence The plan should be sustainable enough to repeat week after week.
Satiety High-satiety foods make long-term compliance much easier.
